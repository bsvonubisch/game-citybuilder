# Art direction and provenance

Selected: **Pine & Copper** architecture and **Garden Streets (C)** environment treatment.

Terrain update selected: **Alpine Slate (C)** roads, meadow grass and deposits; **Clear Brook (A)** water and shoreline palette.

All raster artwork in `public/` was generated with the built-in image generation tool for this project. The runtime frames the atlases directly; it does not use the mockup images as a fake playable world. Roads, terrain, and overlays are rendered procedurally. Assets are provisional game art and can be replaced independently of simulation data.

## Ground alignment

`src/building-art.ts` records the left, front and right outer foundation corners for all 52 building/house-tier views. Atlases include ground-level props inside a stone platform. Calibration sizes are 627×627 for most buildings (including both upgraded houses), 768×512 for sawmill and warehouse views, and 443×443 for the top-row quarry/mine views. Runtime source sizes are normalized to these reference coordinates. Rotations face southwest, southeast, northeast and northwest; corresponding frontage tiles govern road access.

`plot-house-2.png` is the three-storey Townhouse; `plot-house-3.png` is the four-storey Grand house. Both are 2×2 atlases with front and rear views, generated with the built-in image tool from the cottage style reference. Prompts, including the townhouse atlas-layout correction, are saved in `house-tiers-prompts.json`. Houses retain the same 2×2 occupied lot at every tier.

Building sprites now use a single uniform scale and translation. There is no projective warp, shear, or unequal axis scaling: distances, angles, roof slopes and wall proportions are preserved everywhere in each sprite. A separate flat Alpine Slate lot fills the exact occupied grid. This avoids stretching near-square artwork onto rectangular plots. Animation anchors use the identical rigid transform.

Garden fences use sixteen cached connection variants textured with the existing bridge timber; only flat rail geometry is projected. Adjacent cardinal fence tiles share edge endpoints and join without gaps. No additional raster asset was generated for fences.

Slate is projected to exact 2:1 tiles; grass and water use continuous screen-space fields so blades and ripples retain their camera orientation. Terrain is baked after edits at four source pixels per world pixel, in chunks no larger than 2048 pixels. Pebbles, shallows, curbs, and reeds dress the edges. Bridges use textured decks, shared ramp heights for agents and geometry, support posts, and independently depth-sorted railings. Their openings follow road connections. The generation prompts for the new materials are recorded in `terrain-hd-prompts.json`.

Water glints, fountain droplets, chimney puffs, saw motion, conveyor cargo, and wheel spokes are small runtime overlays. Machinery requires an enabled, staffed, road-connected factory with inputs and output capacity; pauses freeze its phase. Windmill rotation uses a cached atlas rather than rerasterizing the sails each frame. Pedestrian gait follows traveled distance, and all animation timing uses simulation speed.

`plotTransform` fits the original foundation inside both plot axes without deforming the artwork. `PlotArt` caches the paving and uniformly scaled sprite at four pixels per world pixel, using one draw for the whole sprite. Preview and finished buildings share this renderer. New rectangular plots swap dimensions at quarter turns; legacy plots keep their original dimensions until explicitly rotated. Elevated roofs and canopies may occlude neighbouring ground. Small standalone resource deposits also use `fitGround`.

Water uses a moving TileSprite beneath the cached shoreline mask, so animation does not rebake terrain. Bridge support pilings are drawn before all decks; upper railing posts stop at deck height. This prevents supports showing through planks. Trees and street props are individual persisted decorations, not regenerated on every edit.

For a visual review, run the dev server and open `/tests/footprints.html` (optionally `?kind=sawmill`, or `?kind=house&tier=3`). It shows each view surrounded by roads, with lot, original foundation and entrance guides. This audit is excluded from the production build. Recalibrate landmarks when replacing artwork; verify containment and uniform-scale regression checks. The original building prompts are recorded in `building-plots-prompts.json`.

## Prompt briefs

The original style comparison requested three equal panels of the same cozy isometric city, with visible cars, inhabitants, factories and supply chains: sunny Honeyfield, forest Pine & Copper, pastel Sugar Canal. Crisp detailed SNES-era pixel art, 2:1 camera.

The building atlas requested nine isolated alpha sprites in a 3×3 grid with consistent timber construction, dark green shingle roofs, amber windows, copper equipment, upper-left light and 2:1 isometric perspective.

The environment comparison kept the approved cottage architecture constant and requested detailed trees, villagers, vehicles and seamless roads in three treatments. Garden Streets specifies lush oak/pine foliage, warm gray paving, gravel sidewalks, flower planters, dimensional villagers and vintage compact cars.

The final garden atlas used the approved building atlas and street comparison as references. It requested sixteen isolated alpha sprites in a regular 4×4 grid: mature oak, textured spruce, birch, flowering tree; four clothed villagers in different directions; forest-green and ochre compact cars, red plank pickup, cream/green delivery truck; planter, bench/copper lantern, mossy rocks, timber gate. All assets use crisp shaded pixel texture, upper-left warm light, and the approved house palette.

The directional atlases used the approved assets as references. Each vehicle/person row contains the same subject facing southeast, southwest, northwest, and northeast, with genuine front and rear views. The cottage atlas requests four 90-degree world rotations of the same architecture, retaining chimney, timber, windows, and roof style. All three sheets use real transparency, isolated grid cells, consistent pixel shading, and a fixed isometric camera.
