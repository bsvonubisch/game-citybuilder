# Art direction and provenance

Before generating new sprites, follow [Sprite acceptance standard](SPRITE-STANDARDS.md).
It defines the required 2:1 geometry, physical scale, four-view consistency and
in-game acceptance checks. Older prompts below are provenance, not replacements
for that standard.

Selected: **Pine & Copper** architecture and **Garden Streets (C)** environment treatment.

Terrain update selected: **Alpine Slate (C)** roads, meadow grass and deposits; **Clear Brook (A)** water and shoreline palette.

All raster artwork in `public/` was generated with the built-in image generation tool for this project. The runtime frames the atlases directly; it does not use the mockup images as a fake playable world. Roads, terrain, and overlays are rendered procedurally. Assets are provisional game art and can be replaced independently of simulation data.

## Ground alignment

`src/building-art.ts` records the left, front and right outer foundation corners for all 52 building/house-tier views. Atlases include ground-level props inside a stone platform. Calibration sizes are 627×627 for most buildings (including both upgraded houses and the replacement warehouse), 768×512 for sawmill views, and 443×443 for the top-row quarry/mine views. Runtime source sizes are normalized to these reference coordinates. Rotations face southwest, southeast, northeast and northwest; corresponding frontage tiles govern road access.

`plot-warehouse-v2.png` and `plot-park-v2.png` replace the original skewed warehouse and fountain foundations. All four views were regenerated with the built-in image tool from their original sheets, requesting a fixed orthographic 2:1 camera, upright verticals, tidy thin stone foundations and transparent backgrounds. Warehouse prompt: preserve the pine roof, timber walls, copper pipe, loading ramp and cargo; four equal atlas cells with two front and two rear views of the same square-plan building. Fountain prompt: preserve the round stone fountain, turquoise water, benches, copper lanterns, flowerboxes and evergreen; rotate their arrangement across four views on identical symmetrical 2:1 diamond bases. Neither atlas is sheared or stretched at runtime; the warehouse's extra occupied space remains a flat loading apron. Original sheets are retained for provenance. The footprint review page shows each view beside road tiles.

`plot-house-2.png` is the three-storey Townhouse; `plot-house-3.png` is the four-storey Grand house. Both are 2×2 atlases with front and rear views, generated with the built-in image tool from the cottage style reference. Prompts, including the townhouse atlas-layout correction, are saved in `house-tiers-prompts.json`. Houses retain the same 2×2 occupied lot at every tier.

Building sprites now use a single uniform scale and translation. There is no projective warp, shear, or unequal axis scaling: distances, angles, roof slopes and wall proportions are preserved everywhere in each sprite. A separate flat Alpine Slate lot fills the exact occupied grid. This avoids stretching near-square artwork onto rectangular plots. Animation anchors use the identical rigid transform.

Garden fences use sixteen cached connection variants textured with the existing bridge timber; only flat rail geometry is projected. Adjacent cardinal fence tiles share edge endpoints and join without gaps. No additional raster asset was generated for fences.

Slate is projected to exact 2:1 tiles; grass and water use continuous screen-space fields so blades and ripples retain their camera orientation. Terrain is baked after edits at four source pixels per world pixel, in chunks no larger than 2048 pixels. Pebbles, shallows, curbs, and reeds dress the edges. Bridges use textured decks, shared ramp heights for agents and geometry, support posts, and independently depth-sorted railings. Their openings follow road connections. The generation prompts for the new materials are recorded in `terrain-hd-prompts.json`.

Water glints, fountain droplets, chimney puffs, saw motion, conveyor cargo, and wheel spokes are small runtime overlays. Machinery requires an enabled, staffed, road-connected factory with inputs and output capacity; pauses freeze its phase. Windmill rotation uses a cached atlas rather than rerasterizing the sails each frame. Pedestrian gait follows traveled distance, and all animation timing uses simulation speed.

`plotTransform` fits the original foundation inside both plot axes without deforming the artwork. `PlotArt` caches the paving and uniformly scaled sprite at four pixels per world pixel, using one draw for the whole sprite. Preview and finished buildings share this renderer. New rectangular plots swap dimensions at quarter turns; legacy plots keep their original dimensions until explicitly rotated. Elevated roofs and canopies may occlude neighbouring ground. Small standalone resource deposits also use `fitGround`.

Water uses a moving TileSprite beneath the cached shoreline mask, so animation does not rebake terrain. Bridge support pilings are drawn before all decks; upper railing posts stop at deck height. This prevents supports showing through planks. Trees and street props are individual persisted decorations, not regenerated on every edit.

For a visual review, run the dev server and open `/tests/footprints.html` (optionally `?kind=sawmill`, or `?kind=house&tier=3`). It shows each view surrounded by roads, with lot, original foundation and entrance guides. This audit is excluded from the production build. Recalibrate landmarks when replacing artwork; verify containment and uniform-scale regression checks. The original building prompts are recorded in `building-plots-prompts.json`.

## Prompt briefs

The original style comparison requested three equal panels of the same cozy isometric city, with visible cars, inhabitants, factories and supply chains: sunny Honeyfield, forest Pine & Copper, pastel Sugar Canal. Crisp detailed SNES-era pixel art, 2:1 camera.

The building atlas requested nine isolated alpha sprites in a 3×3 grid with consistent timber construction, dark green shingle roofs, amber windows, copper equipment, upper-left light and 2:1 isometric perspective.

The environment comparison kept the approved cottage architecture constant and requested detailed trees, villagers, vehicles and seamless roads in three treatments. Garden Streets specifies lush oak/pine foliage, warm gray paving, gravel sidewalks, flower planters, dimensional villagers and vintage compact cars.

The final garden atlas used the approved building atlas and street comparison as references. It requested sixteen isolated alpha sprites in a regular 4×4 grid: mature oak, textured spruce, birch, flowering tree; four clothed villagers in different directions; forest-green and ochre compact cars, red plank pickup, cream/green delivery truck; planter, bench/copper lantern, mossy rocks, timber gate. All assets use crisp shaded pixel texture, upper-left warm light, and the approved house palette.

The directional atlases used the approved assets as references. Each vehicle/person row contains the same subject facing southeast, southwest, northwest, and northeast, with genuine front and rear views. The cottage atlas requests four 90-degree world rotations of the same architecture, retaining chimney, timber, windows, and roof style. All three sheets use real transparency, isolated grid cells, consistent pixel shading, and a fixed isometric camera.

## Copper Mug tavern

`public/building-tavern.png` is an original transparent 2×2 atlas generated with the built-in image tool using `building-warehouse.png` as a style reference. Four quarter-turn views use the same timber/plaster building, teal roof, copper mug sign, warm windows and barrels. The renderer adds the shared stone foundation; it uses uniform scaling and translation only, never shearing the artwork.

Prompt: Use case: stylized-concept. Production isometric city-builder sprite atlas. Reference is STYLE ONLY. Generate one identical cozy village tavern viewed from four successive quarter-turn directions in a transparent 2×2 atlas. High-detail crisp cozy pixel art matching the warehouse: dark teal shingle roof, honey timber, cream plaster, amber windows, copper lanterns. Compact square plan, two storeys, simple gabled roof, brick chimney, central front door with low step, copper mug sign without text, small flower boxes, two barrels tight against the wall. Top left front door faces lower-left, top right lower-right, bottom left and bottom right are corresponding rear views. Same architecture and dimensions throughout. Clean 2:1 ground axes, upright verticals, no perspective convergence. Narrow wall footings only; no raised platform, paving, lawn, outside shadows, people or tables. Equal cells, equal scale, transparent padding, no clipping/labels/grid. True transparency.

Generated source: `exec-5ca54c85-3eb9-4776-b8a7-4a309fb87544.png`. Runtime window/lantern glow is a cached light-emission mask over existing bright amber painted pixels, with depth matching the underlying building; source images are unchanged.

## Intermediate travelling views

`public/vehicles-turns.png` and `public/pedestrian-turns.png` were generated using the built-in image tool, with the existing `vehicles-directions.png` and `pedestrian-walk.png` as their respective identity/style references. Original diagonal atlases are retained. New vehicle columns and pedestrian rows run screen-down, screen-left, screen-up, screen-right. Each is a real yaw view at the same elevated camera angle; the renderer never rotates or skews the upright body. Vehicle scale is calibrated per variant across the entire sheet, not enlarged to a fixed width when viewed head-on. Pedestrians retain four walk phases and fixed scale through the turn.

Vehicle prompt: Use case: stylized-concept. Game sprite atlas, supporting reference image shows our existing 4 vehicle variants in 4 diagonal directions. Create a companion transparent atlas of the SAME vehicles at the FOUR MISSING in-between screen headings. Exactly 4 columns and 4 rows, 16 isolated sprites, equal square cells, clean transparent gaps, no text or labels. Columns left to right: DOWN screen (straight front facing viewer), LEFT screen (exact left side profile), UP screen (straight rear facing away), RIGHT screen (exact right side profile). Camera remains elevated at 30 degrees looking down for ALL views, visible roof, true orthographic isometric game camera. The vehicles stay upright on the ground; these are yaw rotations, never tilted or 2D rotated images. Rows top to bottom: dark olive green vintage compact car, golden yellow vintage compact car, red wooden-plank pickup truck, olive green delivery truck with cream canvas canopy. Match identical design, proportions, materials, warm highlights, cozy detailed pixel-art SNES enhanced aesthetic of reference. Maintain same physical scale across all views in a row; side-on silhouettes naturally longer than front/back. Center vehicle ground-contact midpoint in each equal cell with room around every sprite. Transparent background only, no floor/platform, no checkerboard, no grid lines, no labels.

Pedestrian prompt: Use case: stylized-concept. Game sprite atlas. Reference shows our two existing cozy pixel-art town pedestrians in diagonal directions. Create a companion atlas with the FOUR MISSING screen headings. Transparent background. Exactly 8 columns and 4 rows, 32 isolated sprites in equal cells. Row1 walks DOWN toward viewer (frontal view); row2 walks LEFT (exact left profile); row3 walks UP away from viewer (back view); row4 walks RIGHT (exact right profile). Elevated orthographic game camera looking down 30 degrees, all characters upright, same camera as reference, no perspective changes. Each cycle: left leg forward, passing stance, right leg forward, passing stance. Arms alternate naturally, hat and head stay stable. Preserve character identity, clothes, rich cozy detailed pixel-art materials and proportions from reference. Same character size and ground baseline throughout; shoes center at 85% cellheight, every sprite isolated with transparent margin, no text, no grid, no scene, no shadows extending to adjacent cells, no new accessories.

Generated sources: vehicles `exec-dc83d1ee-e6f8-4567-a350-6e158c72f47b.png`; pedestrians `exec-91f0aeec-9972-40a1-8147-c18675b9016f.png`.
